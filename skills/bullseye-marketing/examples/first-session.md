# Example: starting the interview

This is fictional input and expected behavior, not a measured test result.

## User

```text
Use $bullseye-marketing for ReportNest, a fictional tool that helps small
agencies turn campaign data into client reports. We have a working product,
12 trial users and no paying customers. We can spend £300 and 10 team hours
on the first two-week experiment. I will share a product brief, pricing page
and anonymized trial funnel. Interview me before recommending channels.
```

## Expected first response

The assistant asks 10 tailored questions and requests the promised sources. It should investigate who buys, which reporting pain matters, current alternatives, pricing, the paid-customer goal, the trial funnel, previous attempts, budget scope, team capacity and evidence. It should not treat trial users as paying customers or recommend its three channels before receiving the answers.

## After the answers

Expect a customized brief, a 19-channel table, three candidate experiments and a feasible schedule. With no paid history, CAC values are explicitly modelled scenarios. The £300 cap applies to the first experiment in this example, so the assistant must establish the budget for subsequent tests before committing the team to all three.

## Returning with results

```text
Review this test against our original criteria. We spent £80 and 6 hours,
reached 50 prospects, had 4 qualified conversations and acquired 0 paying
customers. Two conversations are still open. Keep the cohort's sales cycle
in mind and tell me what remains inconclusive.
```

The assistant should not report zero CAC or fill the Inner Ring with a winner. It should separate the observed result from open opportunities and recommend the next evidence-gathering step.
