# Channels and test design

## Outer Ring: all 19 channels

Use these categories once each. Platforms are tactics inside channels, not additional channels. For instance, LinkedIn ads, outbound messages and an owned LinkedIn audience are different acquisition mechanisms.

1. Targeting blogs — reach relevant audiences through other publishers' blogs.
2. Public relations — earn editorial coverage.
3. Unconventional PR — create a distinctive, newsworthy action.
4. Search engine marketing — buy search-intent traffic.
5. Social and display ads — buy audience-targeted placements.
6. Offline ads — use relevant physical, print, radio or other offline inventory.
7. Search engine optimization — earn relevant organic search visibility.
8. Content marketing — attract buyers through useful owned content.
9. Email marketing — nurture an accessible, permissioned email audience.
10. Viral marketing — design product sharing or referrals into acquisition.
11. Engineering as marketing — offer a useful tool that attracts buyers.
12. Business development — form distribution or commercial partnerships.
13. Sales — directly prospect, qualify and close buyers.
14. Affiliate programs — reward third parties for attributable acquisition.
15. Existing platforms — acquire through relevant marketplaces or platforms.
16. Trade shows — access a concentrated industry audience.
17. Offline events — host or join relevant in-person gatherings.
18. Speaking engagements — teach or present to a relevant audience.
19. Community building — build sustained interaction around a shared need.

Create an Outer Ring table with channel, one startup-specific idea, why it could work, evidence/assumption, and feasibility. Even low-fit channels get an idea, followed by an honest reason to deprioritize them.

## Middle Ring: select three

Compare customer concentration and access, buying intent, economics, speed to useful evidence, team advantage and scale potential. Explain tradeoffs rather than disguising guesses as precise weighted scores. A candidate without verified presence may be selected as a discovery test, explicitly labelled provisional. Explain why the strongest alternatives missed the cut.

For each selected candidate include:

- Segment, offer, conversion path and falsifiable acquisition hypothesis.
- Customer-presence evidence: dated source, observable segment match and access conditions. Membership counts, search volume and competitor activity suggest presence; they do not establish buying intent or reachable unique customers.
- Total relevant channel audience estimate and realistic two-week reachable audience, separately. State geography, eligibility, overlap, access limits, method and uncertainty. If unmeasurable, say unknown and specify how to estimate it.
- CAC scenario range and assumptions, anticipated cash/time, dependencies, confidence and major failure risk.

### Economics and attribution

Cash CAC = attributable acquisition cash spend / new attributable paying customers. Include relevant media, tooling, creative, fees and incentives. Show labor hours separately; calculate fully loaded CAC only with an explicit hourly cost assumption. Avoid double-counting shared costs and customers.

Before outcomes exist, model scenarios: expected paid customers = reachable prospects × relevant funnel conversion rates. Divide scenario cost by scenario paid customers. State each rate's source or assumption and show conservative/base/optimistic cases when useful. CPL, CPC, signups and meetings are not CAC. With zero paid customers, observed CAC is undefined and no channel has demonstrated economic success. Never report zero CAC from zero customers.

Choose a consistent attribution rule, conversion window and paid-customer definition before testing. Use tagged links, CRM source fields, campaign codes and payment evidence as appropriate; deduplicate customers and document multi-touch ambiguity. Report conversion cohorts and windows so immature tests are not compared as if complete. Do not infer registrations or purchases from observed accounts or clicks.

## Two-week experiment deliverable

For each candidate, specify hypothesis, segment, offer, minimum viable tactic, cash cap, labor cap, dependencies, primary paid outcome, diagnostic funnel metrics and attribution setup. Set proposed success, stop, continuation and inconclusive thresholds before execution. Derive economic thresholds from the user's economics; if unknown, call thresholds provisional and avoid a profitability claim. State a practical minimum exposure or sample needed for interpretation, without claiming statistical certainty from a tiny sample.

Provide 14 rows (Day 1 through Day 14) per test: action, deliverable/exposure, time/cost and metric to record. Make actions specific to that channel. Include instrumentation checks, launch, measurement, bounded iteration and final review. Preserve the original hypothesis and log changes so an offer change is not mistaken for channel evidence.

Roll up total cash and hours across all three tests, including shared preparation, against the available caps. Use calendar dates only when a start date is supplied or explicitly assumed. Where deadlines conflict with capacity, surface that conflict and propose a feasible sequence.

SEO, partnerships, enterprise sales and other slow channels may produce only early signals in 14 days. Design a useful short validation test, name the lag and next observation window, and avoid either declaring a winner from leads or declaring failure just because sales have not matured.

Use a review table: channel, spend, hours, exposure, qualified leads, paying customers, revenue, observed CAC, evidence maturity, decision and next action. If none wins, leave the Inner Ring empty and identify whether to improve evidence, offer, audience or the next channel hypothesis.
